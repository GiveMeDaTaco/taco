# Internal Communications Skill

## When to use this skill

To write internal communications, use this skill for:
- 3P updates (Progress, Plans, Problems)
- Company newsletters
- FAQ responses
- Status reports
- Leadership updates
- Project updates
- Incident reports

## How to use this skill

1. **Identify the communication type** from the request
2. **Follow the appropriate guideline section** below
3. If the type doesn't match any section, follow the General Comms section and ask for clarification

---

## 3P Updates (Progress, Plans, Problems)

### What they are

3P updates stand for "Progress, Plans, Problems." The main audience is executives, leadership, and teammates. They're meant to be very succinct and to-the-point — something you can read in 30–60 seconds or less, written for people with some but not deep context on what the team does.

3Ps cover a team of any size. The bigger the team, the less granular the tasks. A "mobile team" might note "shipped feature" or "fixed bugs"; a company-wide 3P might say "hired 20 new people" or "closed 10 new deals."

They represent the work of a team across one week and include three sections:
1. **Progress** — what the team accomplished. Focus on things shipped, milestones achieved, tasks completed.
2. **Plans** — what the team intends to do next week. Focus on top priorities.
3. **Problems** — anything slowing the team down (too few people, blockers, deals that fell through, etc.).

Before writing, confirm the team name if not specified.

### Formatting (strict — do not deviate)

```
[emoji] [Team Name] ([Date Range, usually one week])
Progress: [1–3 sentences]
Plans: [1–3 sentences]
Problems: [1–3 sentences]
```

Pick an emoji that captures the vibe of the team and the update. Each section: max 1–3 sentences, clear and to the point, data-driven with metrics where possible. Tone should be matter-of-fact, not prose-heavy.

### Information sources to pull from

- **Slack**: posts from team members, especially in large channels with lots of reactions
- **Google Drive**: docs from key team members with high view counts
- **Email**: emails with lots of replies or substantive content for the relevant time period
- **Calendar**: important non-recurring meetings (product reviews, etc.)

Time scope: Progress and Problems = past week; Plans = next week.

## Company Newsletter

### What it is

A company-wide newsletter summarizing the past week or month. Audience: entire company. Length: ~20–25 bullet points. Distributed via Slack and email.

### Key attributes

- **Lots of links**: Google Drive docs, prominent Slack messages in announce channels, executive posts, significant emails
- **Short and to-the-point**: each bullet ~1–2 sentences
- **Use "we" tense**: "we did this," "we did that"

### Information sources

- **Slack**: messages in large channels with high reaction/response counts
- **Email**: company-wide announcements from executives
- **Calendar**: large-attendee meetings (All-Hands, major announcements); docs attached to those meetings
- **Documents**: new docs published in the past 1–2 weeks with high attention (company vision, quarterly plans, authored by key executives)
- **External press**: references to articles or coverage the company received

### Prioritization

Focus on:
- Company-wide impact (not team-specific details)
- Leadership announcements
- Major milestones and achievements
- Information that affects most employees
- External recognition or press

Avoid:
- Overly granular team updates (those belong in 3Ps)
- Information only relevant to small groups
- Duplicate information already communicated

### Format example

```
:megaphone: Company Announcements
- Announcement 1
- Announcement 2

:dart: Progress on Priorities
- Area 1
    - Sub-area 1
    - Sub-area 2
- Area 2
    - Sub-area 1
    - Sub-area 2

:pillar: Leadership Updates
- Post 1
- Post 2

:thread: Social / External Updates
- Update 1
- Update 2
```

Break content into themed sections (e.g., Product Development / Go-to-Market / Finance, or Recruiting / Execution / Vision). Choose sections that fit the company's activity that week.

## FAQ Responses

### What they are

A summary of frequently asked questions circulating across the company, with clear answers. Goal: reduce confusion and keep employees informed on things that affect a large portion of the workforce.

Areas of interest typically include: recent corporate events (fundraising, new executives), upcoming launches, hiring progress, changes to vision or focus.

### Information sources

- **Slack**: questions asked with lots of reactions or thread responses (signals widespread curiosity)
- **Email**: FAQs written directly or inferable from executive communications
- **Documents**: Google Drive docs, calendar-linked docs — infer FAQs from their contents

### Formatting

```
- *Question*: [1 sentence]
- *Answer*: [1–2 sentences]
```

### Answer guidelines

- Base answers on official company communications when possible
- If information is uncertain, indicate that clearly
- Link to authoritative sources (docs, announcements, emails)
- Keep tone professional but approachable
- Flag if a question requires executive input or an official response
- Be holistic — capture the whole company, not just the user's team

## General Internal Communications

For anything that doesn't match the above formats (status reports, leadership updates, project updates, incident reports, etc.).

### Before writing, clarify

1. **Target audience** — who will read this?
2. **Purpose** — what should the reader know or do after reading?
3. **Tone** — formal, casual, urgent, or informational?
4. **Formatting requirements** — any specific structure expected?

### General principles

- Be clear and concise
- Use active voice
- Put the most important information first
- Include relevant links and references
- Match the company's established communication style
